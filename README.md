# Awesome Scam Defense

**Scams work because they rush you. This list slows them down.**

A curated, action-first defense kit against scams, fraud and social engineering — the tools and plays a normal person can actually use: verify the thing in front of you, harden the accounts they target first, and know exactly what to do in the first 72 hours when one lands.

**Breachnode Field Guide № 1.** Maintained by [Breachnode](https://github.com/Breachnode) — a U.S. Navy veteran and small-business operator who has fought fraud from the victim's side of the table.

> **The border of this list:** prevention, in-the-moment verification, and emergency containment live here. The long institutional fight — credit disputes, bureau blocks, complaint escalations, data-broker removal — lives in the sister list, **Consumer Revenge Kit** *(in development)*. Veterans: your dedicated list is **Veteran Arsenal** *(in development)*.

## 🚨 In the Moment: Is This a Scam?

Sixty seconds of checking beats sixty days of cleanup. Run the thing they sent you through these before you act on it.

- **[VirusTotal](https://www.virustotal.com)** — Paste any link or upload any file; 70+ security scanners vote on it. If it's dirty, walk away. `Free`
- **[urlscan.io](https://urlscan.io)** — See what a link *actually* does — screenshots, redirects, the real destination — without opening it yourself. `Free`
- **[Have I Been Pwned](https://haveibeenpwned.com)** — Check whether your email or phone number appeared in a known breach. If it has, "we've detected a problem with your account" texts are working from real data about you. `Free`
- **[ICANN Lookup](https://lookup.icann.org)** — Check when the domain behind a "bank" or "support" site was registered. A bank site created last Tuesday is not a bank site. `Free`
- **[ScamAdviser](https://www.scamadviser.com)** — Paste a shopping site and get a trust read built from registration age, hosting, and reports. Young site + luxury prices + no address = close the tab. `Free`
- **[BBB Scam Tracker](https://www.bbb.org/scamtracker)** — Search the exact pitch, name or number you were given. Scams run in waves; someone has usually reported yours already. `Free`
- **[TinEye](https://tineye.com)** — Reverse-search a seller's or "agent's" photos. Stolen photos mean a stolen identity and a listing that doesn't exist. `Free`
- **[PhishTank](https://phishtank.com)** — Community-verified database of live phishing sites. If the link you got is in here, it has already burned someone else. `Free`
- **[Chainabuse](https://www.chainabuse.com)** — Check whether a crypto wallet address, site or "exchange" has been reported for fraud before you send anything irreversible. `Free`
- **[AbuseIPDB](https://www.abuseipdb.com)** — Check whether an IP address that's been contacting your server, site or inbox is a known scanner or abuser. `Free`

## 📞 Calls & Texts

Robocalls and scam texts depend on you picking up and staying on the line. Starve them.

- **[National Do Not Call Registry](https://www.donotcall.gov)** — Register your number. Legitimate telemarketers must stop; scammers won't — which makes any sales call after registration an instant tell. `Free`
- **[Nomorobo](https://www.nomorobo.com)** — Blocks calls from a constantly updated database of known robocallers; strongest on landlines and VoIP. `Freemium`
- **[YouMail](https://www.youmail.com)** — Robocall blocking plus visual voicemail, backed by one of the largest spam-call databases in the U.S. `Freemium`
- **[Hiya](https://www.hiya.com)** — Caller ID that labels spam and scam risk before you answer. `Freemium`
- **[RoboKiller](https://www.robokiller.com)** — Blocks spam calls and wastes robocallers' time with answer bots, so your number becomes expensive to dial. `Paid`
- **Your carrier's built-in blocker** — [T-Mobile Scam Shield](https://www.t-mobile.com/benefits/scam-shield), [AT&T ActiveArmor](https://www.att.com/security/activearmor/), [Verizon Call Filter](https://www.verizon.com/solutions-and-services/call-filter/). Already in your plan, probably switched off. Turn it on. `Freemium`
- **[Google Voice](https://voice.google.com)** — A free second number for forms, listings and strangers. When it gets burned by spam, your real number hasn't. `Freemium`
- **[MySudo](https://mysudo.com)** — Private alias phone numbers and emails you can hand out instead of your own — one for selling, one for signups, none of them you. `Paid`
- 📋 **Silence unknown callers** — Turn on iPhone's Silence Unknown Callers or Android's call screening. Real people leave a message; robocallers hang up. The quiet is the feature. `Free`
- 📋 **The Callback Rule** — Any call or text asking for money, codes, or "verification" gets the same response: hang up, and call the number printed on your card or statement. Never call back the number in a suspicious voicemail. The real institution will never mind. `Free`

## 📧 Phishing: Texts & Emails

The delivery text, the toll notice, the "your package is held" alert — the bait is always a small fee or a quick login. The fee is never the point; your card and credentials are.

- 📋 **Forward it, then delete it** — Forward phishing emails to **reportphish@apwg.org** and scam texts to **7726** (SPAM). Your carrier and the Anti-Phishing Working Group use them to kill the campaign. Then delete — don't click anything in it, including "unsubscribe," which only confirms your number is live. `Free`
- **[Report a phishing page to Google Safe Browsing](https://safebrowsing.google.com/safebrowsing/report_phish/)** — One short form gets a confirmed phishing site flagged in Chrome, Gmail and Android warnings for everyone else too. `Free`
- 📋 **Preview before you tap** — Long-press (phone) or hover (computer) on any link to see the real destination before opening it. If the address isn't the company's actual domain, nothing else about the message matters. `Free`
- 📋 **Read the sender's domain letter by letter** — Scam mail comes from lookalike domains: amaz0n, paypa1, your bank's name plus a hyphen and a word. Your eyes autocomplete the real brand; slow down and spell it. `Free`
- 📋 **The delivery-fee rule** — USPS, UPS and FedEx do not text you to pay a redelivery fee or "confirm" your address to release a package. Tracking happens on their sites, with a tracking number you already have. `Free`
- 📋 **The toll-text rule** — E-ZPass, SunPass and state toll agencies do not collect tolls through text-message links. Every "unpaid toll, pay now or face penalties" text is bait. `Free`
- 📋 **The QR code rule** — A stickered QR code on a parking meter, menu or letter can send you to a fake payment page. Type the address yourself, or pay at the meter/machine directly. `Free`

## 🎭 AI & Deepfake Scams

Three seconds of your voice — from any public video — is enough to clone it. The defense isn't better ears; it's better protocol.

- 📋 **The Family Code Word** — Agree on one word with your family that no scammer can guess. Any "emergency" call — a crying grandchild, a "lawyer," a "kidnapper" — must produce the word or the money conversation is over. Set it up *before* you need it. `Free`
- 📋 **The Memory Question** — Ask something only the real person knows and that isn't on their social media. Cloned voices know your name and your relationships; they don't know what you had for dinner at grandma's in 2019. `Free`
- 📋 **Verify on a second channel** — The "person" calling is one channel. Text their real number, call the office line, message a family group chat. A scammer controls exactly one channel — never all of them. `Free`
- 📋 **The 10-minute rule** — Urgency is the weapon; almost no real emergency expires in ten minutes. Any request that can't survive a ten-minute verification pause is a scam until proven otherwise. `Free`
- 📋 **The wave test** — On a live video call you suspect, ask them to turn their head sideways or wave a hand across their face. Real-time face-swap filters glitch on exactly those movements. `Free`
- **[Deepware Scanner](https://scanner.deepware.ai)** — Upload a suspicious video and get a deepfake-likelihood read before you act on what it shows. `Free`
- **[InVID Verification Plugin](https://www.invid-project.eu/tools-and-services/invid-verification-plugin/)** — Browser plugin built for journalists to verify viral videos: keyframe search, metadata, and context checks in one place. `Free`

## 🛒 Marketplace & Money Movement

Most marketplace fraud dies the moment you refuse the payment method the scammer insists on.

- 📋 **The Gift Card Rule** — No government agency, bank, utility, or employer collects debts, fees or bail in gift cards, wire transfers or crypto. The request *is* the scam, every time. `Free`
- 📋 **The Cash Rule** — Treat Zelle, Venmo and Cash App like handing over cash: fine for people you know in real life, gone forever with everyone else. "Overpayment" and "accidental deposit" tricks both end with you sending real money against a fake payment. `Free`
- 📋 **Goods & Services, never Friends & Family** — Paying a seller through "friends and family" deletes your purchase protection to save them a fee. That saving is the entire point of the ask. `Free`
- 📋 **A screenshot is not a ticket** — Event tickets bought as screenshots can be sold ten times and cancelled once. Buy through the venue or an official resale channel with a guaranteed transfer, or don't go. `Free`
- 📋 **No deposit on a place you haven't stood in** — Rental listings copy real photos from real listings. If you can't tour it (or send someone who can), the below-market rent is the bait, not the deal. `Free`
- **[Privacy.com](https://privacy.com)** — Virtual payment cards with per-merchant spending limits. A card leaked in a breach or used on a sketchy site can't drain your real account. `Freemium`
- **[Escrow.com](https://www.escrow.com)** — For big person-to-person purchases — vehicles, equipment, domains — a licensed escrow holds the money until the goods check out. Never wire a stranger directly. `Paid`
- **[NICB VINCheck](https://www.nicb.org/vincheck)** — Free total-loss and theft check on a used vehicle's VIN before you pay for it. A washed title is a five-figure surprise. `Free`
- **[Protect Your Move (FMCSA)](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move)** — The federal checklist for hiring movers: verify the company's registration, get written estimates, and recognize the "hostage load" pattern before your furniture is on the truck. `Free`
- 📋 **Safe Exchange Zones** — Many police departments run monitored, camera-covered meetup spots for marketplace deals. Search your city + "safe exchange zone" before meeting a stranger with cash or goods. `Free`

## 💼 Job & Money-Mule Scams

A fake job doesn't just waste your time — it recruits you as the laundering layer, or empties your account with a fake check. This section is written from the applicant's side of the table.

- **[FTC: Job Scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams)** — The FTC's field guide to fake openings, fake recruiters and fake checks, including the exact scripts in circulation. Read it before your next application round. `Free`
- 📋 **The upfront-fee rule** — A real employer never charges you to start: not for training, equipment, background checks, or a "certification assessment" you must buy first. Any fee between you and the job means the fee *is* the job — theirs. `Free`
- 📋 **The equipment-check rule** — "We'll send a check; buy your laptop from our vendor" means the check is fake, the vendor is them, and the bank will claw the money back from *your* account. Real companies ship equipment or reimburse after hire. `Free`
- 📋 **The task-work rule** — Jobs that pay you to like posts, rate products, forward packages or "optimize" items — especially ones that start requiring deposits to unlock bigger tasks — are mule work and stolen-goods handling wearing a job costume. `Free`
- 📋 **The interview-channel tell** — Legitimate companies interview on their own systems, with people whose names check out. A hiring process that lives entirely in Telegram, WhatsApp or text messages is avoiding records on purpose. `Free`
- 📋 **Verify the recruiter like they'd verify you** — The posting should exist on the company's real careers page, and the recruiter's email domain should match the company's. A Gmail address and a listing that exists nowhere else is a hard stop. `Free`

## 🏦 Bank, Tax & Government Impersonation

The caller ID says your bank, the IRS, the Sheriff. Caller ID is a costume — it can say anything. Judge the request, never the display.

- 📋 **The "safe account" rule** — Your bank will never ask you to move money to a new or "safe" account to protect it. That instruction *is* the theft, narrated by the thief. Hang up and call the number on your card. `Free`
- 📋 **The IRS rule** — The IRS opens by mail, not by phone, and never demands payment in gift cards, wire or crypto, and never threatens instant arrest. A voicemail doing any of those is a recording of a crime, not a tax notice. `Free`
- **[Get an Identity Protection PIN (IRS)](https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin)** — A six-digit PIN only you and the IRS know, required on your tax return. With one set, nobody else can file a return in your name and steal the refund. `Free`
- **[my Social Security](https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount)** — Claim your own account before a scammer opens one in your name, and check the earnings record they'd love to borrow. `Free`
- **[Report IRS impersonation (TIGTA)](https://www.tigta.gov)** — The Treasury Inspector General tracks tax-scam callers specifically. Two minutes of reporting gives investigators the numbers to shut down. `Free`
- **[U.S. Postal Inspection Service](https://www.uspis.gov)** — Mail fraud, fake check mailings and postal money-order scams are federal crimes with their own police. Report here when the scam arrived by mail. `Free`
- 📋 **The jury-duty rule** — Courts do not collect fines for missed jury duty by phone, and sheriffs do not take payment in gift cards or at kiosks. Missed jury duty is handled by mail and in person, slowly — which is how you know it's real. `Free`
- 📋 **Defeat check washing** — Checks stolen from mailboxes get chemically "washed" and rewritten. Write checks with a gel-ink pen (the ink soaks into the paper and resists washing), and drop them inside the post office — never in a home mailbox with the flag up. `Free`

## 📈 Investment & Crypto Long-Cons

The slow scams don't ask for your password. They ask for your trust, over weeks, and then your savings, in one transfer.

- **[Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov)** — The SEC's investor site: verify whether a person or firm is actually registered, and read the current fraud playbooks in plain English before you wire anything. `Free`
- **[FINRA BrokerCheck](https://brokercheck.finra.org)** — Look up any broker or firm: licenses, employment history, and disciplinary record. An advisor who isn't in here isn't an advisor. `Free`
- 📋 **The pig-butchering pattern** — A friendly stranger (often a wrong number) builds a relationship, casually mentions crypto profits, and steers you to a platform where your balance grows on screen — until withdrawing requires "taxes" or "fees." The platform, the profits and the friend are all the same operation. `Free`
- 📋 **The guaranteed-returns rule** — Guaranteed high returns with low risk do not exist as a retail product. The word "guaranteed" next to a big number is not a feature of the offer; it is the entire fraud. `Free`
- 📋 **The celebrity rule** — A famous face "doubling your crypto" or endorsing a trading platform in a video is an AI edit until you verify it on the person's own official channel. Fame is the cheapest costume deepfakes own. `Free`

## 🔐 Accounts They Target First

Scammers don't start with your bank. They start with your email — it's the master key that resets everything else — and your phone number, which receives the codes.

- **[Bitwarden](https://bitwarden.com)** — Password manager with a genuinely free tier. One unique password per account means one breach stops being every breach. `Freemium`
- **[Proton Pass](https://proton.me/pass)** — Password manager with built-in email aliases, from the Proton privacy suite — a solid alternative if you want passwords and aliases under one roof. `Freemium`
- **[YubiKey](https://www.yubico.com)** — A physical security key for the accounts that matter most (email first, then banking). Phishing sites can fake a login page; they can't fake the key in your hand. `Paid`
- **[SimpleLogin](https://simplelogin.io)** — Email aliases: give every site a different address that forwards to you. When one leaks or gets sold, you see exactly who did it — and you can kill that alias without changing your real email. `Freemium`
- **[DuckDuckGo Email Protection](https://duckduckgo.com/email)** — A free forwarding address that strips trackers before mail reaches you, doubling as an alias layer for signups. `Free`
- 📋 **Turn on passkeys where offered** — Passkeys replace passwords with device-held keys that can't be typed into a fake site. Every major account that offers them is offering you phishing immunity for that account. `Free`
- 📋 **Move codes off SMS** — Text-message codes can be stolen by SIM swap. Switch your important accounts to an authenticator app or a security key, and treat SMS as the fallback, never the plan. `Free`
- 📋 **Lock your phone number** — Turn on your carrier's port-out / number-lock protection (Verizon Number Lock, T-Mobile and AT&T equivalents in account security settings). Five minutes now blocks the SIM-swap that empties accounts later. `Free`
- 📋 **Get breach alerts** — Sign up for Have I Been Pwned's free notification service so the next breach involving your email reaches you from them first — not from someone exploiting it. `Free`

## 📱 Device & Network Hygiene

Boring, quiet, and responsible for stopping more scams than any app on this list.

- 📋 **Updates are armor** — Turn on automatic updates for your phone, computer and browser, and let them install. Most mass exploitation rides bugs that were patched months earlier on devices that never restarted. `Free`
- **[uBlock Origin](https://ublockorigin.com)** — The browser blocker that kills malvertising — including the fake "your computer is infected, call this number" popups that launch tech-support scams. `Free`
- **[NextDNS](https://nextdns.io)** — DNS-level filtering for your whole household: known phishing and scam domains simply stop resolving, on every device, before the page can load. `Freemium`
- **[Mullvad VPN](https://mullvad.net)** — A flat-rate, no-account-number VPN for the moments you do need one — public Wi-Fi, untrusted networks. Anonymous signup is the point. `Paid`
- 📋 **Fix your router once** — Change the default admin password, set WPA3 (or WPA2) with a real passphrase, and turn on automatic firmware updates. Your front door deserves a lock that isn't printed on the manufacturer's website. `Free`
- 📋 **Set up Find My before you need it** — Enable Find My (Apple) or Find Hub (Android), remote lock and remote wipe *now*. After a theft is the wrong time to learn these exist. `Free`
- 📋 **Hide lock-screen previews** — If your texts and authenticator codes show on your locked screen, anyone holding your phone holds your second factor. Set notifications to show content only when unlocked. `Free`
- **[Google Security Checkup](https://myaccount.google.com/security-checkup)** — A five-minute guided audit of the account everything else resets through: old devices, third-party access, recovery options. Run it once a year. `Free`

## 🧓 Protect Your Parents & Grandparents

Elders are targeted because they answer the phone, trust uniforms, and were raised polite. Don't lecture them — arm them, and make yourself part of the system.

- **[AARP Fraud Watch Network](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/)** — Free (no membership needed) scam alerts, a helpline staffed by fraud specialists, and plain-English breakdowns of what's currently circulating. `Free`
- **[National Elder Fraud Hotline](https://www.justice.gov/elderjustice)** — The Justice Department's hotline (1-833-372-8311) for reporting elder fraud and finding local help. Put the number on their fridge. `Free`
- **[Eldercare Locator](https://eldercare.acl.gov)** — The federal directory of local aging services — the people who can help in person when a scam has already done damage. `Free`
- 📋 **Be the trusted contact** — Banks and brokerages let an account holder name a trusted contact the firm can call when something looks wrong. Get named. It doesn't give you control of the money — it gives the bank permission to pause and phone you. `Free`
- 📋 **The remote-access rule** — No legitimate company cold-calls about a virus, and no support agent needs a stranger to install AnyDesk or TeamViewer to "fix" it. If a caller asks for remote access, the call is the attack. `Free`

## ⏰ First 72 Hours

Something got through. Speed matters more than perfection — work this list top to bottom.

- 📋 **Change passwords from a clean device** — Start with your email account (the master key), then banking. Use a device that wasn't part of the incident, and log out all other sessions while you're there. `Free`
- 📋 **Write the timeline while it's fresh** — Dates, times, numbers, names, screenshots, transaction IDs. Memory blurs in days; banks, police and the FTC all work from paper. This one habit multiplies the value of everything below. `Free`
- 📋 **Warn your contacts** — If an account was taken, it will message your people next. A two-line warning ("that wasn't me, don't click anything from that account") cuts the scam's second wave off at the knees. `Free`
- **[IdentityTheft.gov](https://identitytheft.gov)** — The FTC's recovery hub: answer a short interview and get a personalized recovery plan plus a formal Identity Theft Report — the document that unlocks your dispute rights. `Free`
- **Freeze your credit — all three, free by law** — [Equifax](https://www.equifax.com/personal/credit-report-services/credit-freeze/) · [Experian](https://www.experian.com/freeze/center.html) · [TransUnion](https://www.transunion.com/credit-freeze). A freeze stops new accounts in your name and does not affect your score. `Free`
- 📋 **Freeze beats monitoring** — Credit monitoring *watches* a theft happen and tells you after. A freeze *prevents* the new account from opening at all. Monitoring is a fine extra; it is never the substitute. `Free`
- **[ChexSystems Freeze](https://www.chexsystems.com)** — The freeze most people miss: stops criminals opening *bank* accounts in your name, which the credit bureaus can't see. `Free`
- **[AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com)** — Pull all three credit reports free and read them like a damage report: anything you don't recognize gets disputed with the Identity Theft Report from IdentityTheft.gov. `Free`
- **[ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov)** — Report the scam itself. Individual reports feel small; in aggregate they're how the FTC finds and prosecutes the operations behind them. `Free`
- **[IC3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov)** — The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center. File here for larger losses and anything involving impersonation of officials — these reports feed real investigations. `Free`
- 📋 **File the police report** — Some banks and insurers require one before they'll move. Bring your timeline. A report number also adds weight to every dispute you file afterward. `Free`
